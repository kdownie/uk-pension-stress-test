Questions

What is a pension stress test? A simulation that runs many possible futures for a pension pot in drawdown, year by year, to show how often the income lasts to your chosen age and what happens when it does not.

How long will my pension last? It depends on the pot size, income target, returns, tax and inflation. Enter your figures to see the share of simulations that fund the full income to your end age.

What return does it assume? By default the FCA's projection rates (COBS 13 Annex 2): 5% nominal less 2% inflation, giving about 2.94% a year after inflation. The Handbook does not prescribe that 5% — it is the highest intermediate rate a firm may use for a personal pension, and the firm is expected to justify it against the actual investments. The lower and higher cases, 2% and 8%, are in the dropdown. You can also set your own.

Does it model UK income tax? Yes, for England/Wales/NI and Scotland. The basic-rate band is stacked on top of whatever personal allowance survives the £100,000 taper, which most calculators get wrong.

Does it model a couple? Yes. Two pots, two State Pensions and two personal allowances, with each year's withdrawal split between the pots to minimise the household tax bill.

Is this financial advice? No. It is not authorised or regulated by the FCA, gives no recommendation, and names no pension product, fund or provider.

What we found building this. The 4% rule is a fact about American data — run the same test on British market history and the equivalent figure is 3.77%. How this works. Each simulation runs one possible future year by year: State Pension arrives if you're old enough, the pot tops your income up to your target after income tax, and whatever is left earns that year's return. Returns are drawn from a lognormal distribution around the assumptions you set. Income tax is computed properly — the basic-rate band sits on top of whatever personal allowance survives the £100,000 taper, which is the bit most calculators get wrong, and Scottish rates are supported. For a couple, each year's withdrawal is split between the two pots so the household pays as little tax as legally possible — using both personal allowances before either partner reaches a higher rate.

What it does not model. Defined benefit pensions and annuities · the money purchase annual allowance · inheritance tax · investment charges · care costs · mortality as a probability rather than a date you pick · anything about your actual circumstances.

On that third one: for deaths on or after 6 April 2027, most unused pension funds count as part of the estate for inheritance tax — sections 66 to 71 of the Finance Act 2026.
